Dear Whiteshield AI Economics team,

For two years I was the Ministry of Economy and Planning's lead researcher on the Saudi labour
market. I built the national unemployment forecasting model, designed a foreign-worker remittance
forecasting system that reconciled administrative data from more than ten government bodies into one
modelled series, and produced the ministry's shadow-economy estimation — quantifying a sector that,
by definition, does not want to be measured. The findings fed into published national strategy
documents and were briefed at the highest levels of government.

I am applying because that is the same work Whiteshield does, and because the way your unit describes
the job is the way I already work: understand the problem, ideate a solution that addresses the actual
need, build it technically, then communicate it with clarity. In government, skipping any one of those
four steps means the model dies in a folder. I have owned all four — from research design and data
collection through model specification in Python, R, and Stata, to the dashboard and the briefing in
front of ministers.

Three things I would bring on day one:

**Labour-market and competitiveness depth, not just method.** Whiteshield's work on labour resilience
and national competitiveness sits directly on top of the indicators I have spent a decade building and
defending. I know where labour data in this region is thin, where administrative series contradict each
other, and which comparisons across GCC states are genuinely valid — the judgement that determines
whether an index measures something real or produces a defensible-looking ranking.

**Working with hard, incomplete, multi-source data.** The remittance and shadow-economy workstreams
were exercises in inference under bad data: incompatible definitions, mismatched frequencies, and
whole segments unobserved. That is the normal condition for the sources your unit works with.

**Regional access.** I am Riyadh-based, native bilingual Arabic and English, with an active network
across Saudi public institutions and the GCC private sector — and I am equally open to the Dubai or
Barcelona base.

I am candid about where I am building: my modelling background is econometric and time-series rather
than deep-learning, and I have not yet worked hands-on with satellite imagery or LLM pipelines. Both
are exactly why this unit interests me, and given how quickly I built pipelines across ten government
bodies with no template to work from, I would expect to be productive on them fast. Your note that you
want people capable of some of this and eager to develop the rest is the reason I am writing rather
than self-selecting out.

I would welcome the chance to talk about where the AI Economics unit is taking this work.
